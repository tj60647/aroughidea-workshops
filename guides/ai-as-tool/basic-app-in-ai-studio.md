# Building a Basic App in AI Studio

*This guide covers step 4 of the [TLDR](/ai-as-tool/tldr), with more explanation.*

---

## What This Is

Google AI Studio is a vibe-coding tool. You describe an app in a chat, AI Studio writes the code, and a preview shows the app running. In this guide, you build a simple app with no AI features, so you can learn how the tool works before you add an agent.

---

## Words You'll See

- **Google AI Studio:** Google's website for trying Gemini models and building apps with them.
- **Gemini:** Google's family of AI models. AI Studio uses them to write your app's code.
- **Preview:** the running version of your app, shown inside AI Studio.
- **AI feature:** a part of an app that sends text to an AI model while someone uses it, such as a chat box. The app in this guide has none.
- **Iterate:** make a change, look at the result, and decide on the next change.

---

## Before You Start

- **A Google account.** Use a personal Google account if your school's account blocks AI Studio.
- **A topic.** The example is a camera obscura explainer. Any simple topic you can check yourself works too.

---

## Step 4: Build a Basic App in AI Studio

1. Open [Google AI Studio](https://aistudio.google.com/) and sign in.
2. Find the area for building apps and start a new app. AI Studio's menus change from time to time. If you can't find it, look for **Build**.
3. Send AI Studio this:

> Build a simple one-page app that explains how a camera obscura works, for someone who has never heard of one. Use a diagram and a short step-by-step explanation. Don't add any AI features.

To use your own topic, change the first sentence of the prompt before you send it.

**What happens:** AI Studio writes the code, and your app appears in a preview. This can take a minute. AI Studio may also describe what it built.

### Try a change

Look at the app as its audience would. Is the explanation clear? Is the diagram right? Then describe one change, in plain words. For example, send AI Studio this:

> Make the diagram larger, and add a label for each part of the camera obscura.

Look at the preview again. Try two or three changes like this, one at a time. This is the loop you'll use for the rest of the workshop: describe, look, decide.

### Look at the code

AI Studio can show the code behind your app. Find it, and find one thing in the code that you can also see in the app, such as a heading. You don't need to understand the rest. Knowing it's there is enough.

---

## Checkpoint

- Your app runs in AI Studio's preview.
- You changed it at least once by describing the change.
- You found the code behind it.

---

## If Something Goes Wrong

**I can't sign in, or AI Studio says it isn't available.** Some school accounts block AI Studio. Sign in with a personal Google account instead.

**The app shows an error instead of a preview.** Send AI Studio this:

> The app shows an error. Explain the problem in plain language, then fix it.

**AI Studio changed more than I asked.** Say what you want kept: "Put the introduction back the way it was, and keep only the new diagram."

**The explanation is wrong.** AI Studio can make factual mistakes. Check the facts yourself, and tell it what to fix.
