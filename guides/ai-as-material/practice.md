# Practice: Iterating on Your Agent

*What to do before [Workshop 3](/ai-as-tool/). Work as a team if you can, or on your own.*

---

## Keep Iterating

Keep working on your letter-writing assistant, and record each round in your [process journal](/practice/journal).

1. **Reorganize your observations** from bodystorming. Group the sticky notes in a new way, and look for patterns you missed.
2. **Describe the role carefully.** Tighten each line of your role card, using [Is This in the Right Place?](/ai-as-material/right-place).
3. **Describe your agent in a system card:** a one-page record of this version's system instructions, knowledge and settings.
4. **Write a test script:** the same list of messages you'll send to every version, taken from one goal card, including the reveal note and the new constraint.
5. **Run the script in [Agent Studio](https://agentstudio.aroughidea.com/),** and write down what happened and what you think about it.
6. **Change your design.** Record the new version in a new system card, start a new session in Agent Studio, and run the same script.

Repeat steps 5 and 6 **at least three times.** Keep every system card, so you can compare versions.

---

## Get Ready to Present

How will you present your agent's design? Plan a short presentation of the decisions you made, why you made them, and how you tested them. Use the three design artifacts: design intent, a system diagram, and design quals. More detail: [design artifacts](/practice/design-artifacts).

---

## Journal Prompts

Write short answers in your [process journal](/practice/journal):

- What changed between your first and your last version, and what caused each change?
- Which behavior was hardest to get right? Why?
- Where did the agent need judgment that you couldn't write as a rule?
- Who is responsible when the agent writes a letter that hurts the user?
- What would you tell someone designing their first agent?

---

## Words You'll See

- **System card:** a one-page record of one version of your agent's design.
- **Test script:** a fixed list of messages you send to each version, so you can compare them fairly.
