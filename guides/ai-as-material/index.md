# AI as a Material: Modeling Behavior

**The designer's goals haven't changed. The material has.** Good design has always been about judgment, trust, control and outcomes. What's new is that the material can now hold judgment, behavior and autonomy itself. Interface design wrote scripts: every state specified, every path designed. An AI system doesn't run a script. It performs, more like an improv actor. So designers now write the rules, limits and intentions that the performance runs on. Designing an agent means designing its behavior, and that takes three things: your design intention, a clear description of the system, and a way to evaluate it.

---

## What You'll Do

- Learn how a language model gets made, and where its behavior comes from.
- Compare the other kinds of models, and what each one learns to predict.
- Act out a letter-writing assistant with other people, and watch its behavior appear.
- Describe that behavior in a role card, then test it as a real agent in [Agent Studio](https://agentstudio.aroughidea.com/).
- Sort your design into the right place: system instructions, knowledge, or design notes.

---

## Before You Start

- **Workshop 1 helps.** [How It Actually Works](/how-it-works/) explains what a language model does with your text. This workshop explains how that model was made. If you watched the 3Blue1Brown video there, ask yourself: what stuck with you, and what surprised you?
- **For the acting exercise,** you need two or three other people, some sticky notes or paper, and pens. Working alone? The [Bodystorming an Agent](/ai-as-material/bodystorming) guide gives you a way to do it with a chatbot.
- **For testing,** you need a computer with a web browser.
- **No programming.** Nothing here asks you to write code.
