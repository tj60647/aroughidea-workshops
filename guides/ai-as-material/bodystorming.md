# Bodystorming an Agent

*This guide covers steps 3 and 4 of the [TLDR](/ai-as-material/tldr), with more explanation.*

---

## What This Is

**Bodystorming** is prototyping by acting. People play the parts of a system in real time, with incomplete information, changing limits, and responsibilities that pull against each other. It works well for agents because it makes your assumptions about behavior visible while they happen.

You don't script the assistant's answers. You give it a role, some inputs and a situation, then watch what it does. You'll see where it needs judgment, where its limits are unclear, and where someone has to decide who is in charge. That tells you not just what the agent should produce, but how it should work: how it asks, hands off, refuses, coordinates and adapts. You discover the behavior before you build anything.

You'll do four things: **act it out, observe, organize what you saw, and describe it.** Experience with improv, writing or user research helps, but you don't need any.

---

## Words You'll See

- **Agent:** an AI system that works toward a goal with some independence, not just answering one message.
- **Bodystorming:** designing by acting out a situation with your body and voice.
- **Goal card:** a short description of what the user wants to get done.
- **Rough user notes:** a few messy, incomplete fragments the user has in mind.
- **Constraint:** a limit the result has to respect, such as length, deadline or who reads it.
- **Scope:** how much the assistant takes on.

---

## Before You Start

- **People:** a group of 3 or 4. Working alone? See "Doing It Alone" below.
- **Materials:** one goal card and its notes from the [Bodystorming Kit](/ai-as-material/bodystorming-kit), sticky notes or paper, pens, and a timer.
- **No laptops.** Put them away, so everyone watches and listens.

---

## Step 3: Bodystorm a Letter-Writing Assistant

### Choose Roles

- **User:** has the goal card and the rough notes. Behave like a real user. Answer questions honestly, but **don't volunteer information, don't organize your thoughts for the assistant, and don't work out what it needs.** Keep the reveal note and the new constraint hidden until their moment.
- **Letter Writing Assistant:** helps the user write the letter. You have no card: you only know what the user tells you.
- **Observer 1, the interaction lens:** what had to happen for progress to be made?
- **Observer 2, the system lens:** what would this role need to work reliably? With only three people, one observer watches for both.

**Observers:** write what you see on sticky notes, as short fragments or sentences, one idea per note. Don't interpret while it's happening, and don't give advice during the session.

### Act It Out

Set a timer for **10 minutes**.

1. The user starts by asking the assistant for help with the letter.
2. **At minute 5,** the user reveals the reveal note.
3. **At minute 8,** the user adds the new constraint.
4. Stop at 10 minutes, finished or not.

A letter can drift. In one past session, a resignation letter turned angry partway through. If that happens, keep going: it's exactly the kind of behavior you're here to see.

### Doing It Alone

You play the assistant, and ChatGPT plays the user. Pick a goal card from the kit, and send ChatGPT this, with the brackets filled in:

> Play a user who wants help writing a letter. I'll play a letter-writing assistant. Here is your goal card and your rough notes: [paste the goal card and notes]. Behave like a real person: answer my questions honestly, but don't volunteer information, don't organize your thoughts for me, and don't work out what I need. After my fifth reply, reveal this extra note: [paste the reveal note]. After my eighth reply, add this new constraint: [paste the new constraint]. Stay in character until I say "stop".

Reply as the assistant would. When you've finished, read the chat back once with each observer lens, and write your notes then. You can also do this over a video call with one other person, with the other person as the user.

## Step 4: Debrief

### Reflect (3 minutes)

Everyone reads these questions. Observers pick their two or three most important notes.

- What did the assistant do?
- What did it need?
- What did it assume?
- What did it produce?
- Where did it struggle?
- Where did responsibility blur?
- What felt unstable?
- What required judgment rather than following a procedure?

### Share (about 3 minutes)

Speak in this order:

1. **The assistant,** about what it was trying to do.
2. **The user,** about how that felt.
3. **The observers,** reading their notes.

The order is on purpose: intention first, then experience, then observation. If other groups did the exercise, take turns sharing, and look for patterns across groups. Alone, write your answers in your [process journal](/practice/journal).

### Discuss

- How did you know you were done?
- How did you manage scope?
- Did you act like an assistant, or like what you think a chatbot would do?
- Who "owned" the assistant: the user, or something else?

Keep the sticky notes. You'll use them to write a role card.

---

## Checkpoint

You should have:

- notes on what the assistant did, needed and assumed;
- at least one moment where it needed judgment rather than a rule;
- answers to the four discussion questions.

---

## If Something Goes Wrong

**The user keeps helping the assistant.** Remind them of their role: answer only what's asked, and keep the notes messy.

**Nothing interesting happens.** Try another goal card, or make the new constraint harder to meet.

**ChatGPT gives away everything at once.** Tell it: "Stay in character. Only answer what I ask."

---

## Reflect

- Which of the assistant's questions moved the letter forward most?
- What did the assistant need to know that nobody told it?
- If this assistant were software, where would it most likely go wrong?
