# Keeping a Process Journal

A process journal is a record of how your work changed over time: what you tried, what happened, and what you decided. It's for you first. Later, it's the source for your [case-study deck](/presenting/case-study-deck) and your portfolio.

---

## Words You'll See

- **Entry:** one dated section of your journal.
- **Machine accessible:** easy for an AI tool to read. Plain text, headings and a shareable link are machine accessible; a photo of handwriting is less so.
- **System diagram:** a picture of how your system works, such as a flowchart of inputs and outputs, a sequence diagram or an architecture map.
- **Key path:** how your prototype pays for AI, such as a free-tier API key or a prepaid one. See [Workshop 4](/sharing-prototypes/).

---

## Choose Where to Keep It

Use any platform or format you like: a Google Doc, a GitHub repository, a FigJam board or a Notion page. If you share a link, check that the people you share it with can open it. Open it in a private browser window to test.

As you choose, think about how machine accessible it will be. You'll often want an AI tool to read your journal, for example to help you outline a deck. Text with clear headings is easy for a tool to read. Images need a short caption to say what they show.

---

## What to Include

- **Dated entries,** at least one for each workshop. Label each entry with its workshop, for example "Workshop 3: AI as a Tool", as well as the date.
- **Text and images:** screenshots, photos, sketches (by hand or digital), code snippets, conversations, or anything else that shows how your work changed.
- **A system diagram** that shows how your system works. Update it when the system changes, and keep the old versions.
- **Your thinking and experiments** in each exercise, shown clearly: what you expected, what you tried, and what you decided.

---

## For Each Experiment

Record:

- **A comparison of conversations.** Put two runs side by side, such as before and after a change to your instructions, or the same question to two models.
- **Addresses.** The link to each prototype or sandbox you used. A QR code is optional.
- **What surprised you, what broke, and what you'd change.**
- **Which key path you used,** if any.

---

## A Simple Entry Template

Copy this into your journal and fill it in for each entry.

```
Workshop [number]: [name], [date]

What I tried:
What I expected:
What happened: [screenshots or pasted conversation]
What surprised me:
What broke:
What I'd change:
Links: [prototype, sandbox, key path]
```

---

## Reflect

- Could someone else follow what you did from your journal alone?
- Which entry would you show first in a portfolio, and why?
