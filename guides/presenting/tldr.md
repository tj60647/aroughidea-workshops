# TLDR: Presenting Your Work

Show what you built, how you built it and what it taught you: build a short case study, present it, give feedback, and look back over the series. Do one step at a time. The links go to the detailed guides.

1. **Build your deck.** Make a case study of at most 7 slides: your process and iteration, your final prototype with a demo link, the outcome, and your reflections. Include your system diagram, a system card excerpt, a before-and-after revision, what broke and what you did about it, and screenshots. To plan it, paste your journal into an AI chat tool, such as ChatGPT, and send:

   > Here is my process journal. Propose an outline for a case-study deck of at most 7 slides about my prototype: what I made and why, how I made it, and what I learned. For each slide, suggest a headline, the screenshot or diagram that should carry it, and no more than 30 words of text. Use only what's in my journal. If something is missing, ask me instead of inventing it.

   Then test the finished deck on a reader, or send the AI tool your slide text with this:

   > Here is my slide outline. Read it as someone who has never seen my project. Then tell me in three sentences what I made, how I made it and why it matters. Name the one thing you didn't understand.

   **Check:** someone can explain back what you made, how and why, and your live link opens in a private browser window. [Deck guide](/presenting/case-study-deck)

2. **Optional: record a demo video.** Keep it to 2 to 3 minutes: what your prototype does, what you gave it, and where it breaks. To plan it, send an AI chat tool your notes and this:

   > Here is what my prototype does, what I give it and where it breaks. Write a script for a demo video of 2 to 3 minutes. Split it into shots, and for each shot say what to show on screen and what to say. Show the working prototype with its inputs and outputs, and include one place where it breaks. Keep my own words where you can.

   **Check:** the video link opens for someone who isn't signed in. [Video guide](/presenting/demo-video)

3. **Present and give feedback.** Present for 3 minutes, talk for 3 minutes, and hand over within 1 minute. When you give feedback, say what you saw, and be specific. Working alone? Record yourself and send it to one or two people.

   **Check:** you finished in 3 minutes and wrote down the feedback you got. [Presenting guide](/presenting/presenting-and-feedback)

4. **Discuss as a group.** Talk about the themes you noticed across the presentations, your insights, and your next steps. To sort a long set of notes, send an AI chat tool your notes and this:

   > Here are my notes from a set of project presentations. Group them into three to five themes. For each theme, name the projects it came from and quote my notes. Don't add themes that aren't in my notes.

   **Check:** you have a short list of themes, insights and next steps. [Discussion guide](/presenting/presenting-and-feedback#step-4-discuss-as-a-group)

5. **Look back over the series.** Recap the five earlier workshops, then think about AI through three frames: as a **tool** you use, as a **material** you build with, and as a **medium**, a new kind of knowledge artifact. Paste your journal into an AI chat tool and send:

   > Here is my process journal. For each workshop entry, tell me whether I was treating AI as a tool, as a material or as a medium, and quote the line that shows it. Where it's unclear, say so.

   **Check:** you can name the frame that best fits your prototype. [Series review](/presenting/series-in-review)
