# Presenting and Giving Feedback

*This guide covers steps 3–4 of the [TLDR](/presenting/tldr), with more explanation.*

---

## What This Is

This is where you share your journey: you present the work you've been doing and what you experienced while exploring it. Then you listen to others, give them useful feedback, and talk as a group about what you noticed.

The format works best in a group of about ten people. Each presenter gets:

- **3 minutes** to present;
- **3 minutes** of conversation;
- **1 minute** to switch to the next presenter.

With ten presenters, that's a little over an hour.

---

## Words You'll See

- **Feedback:** what a listener tells a presenter about their work, so they can improve it.
- **Theme:** an idea or problem that comes up in more than one project.
- **Insight:** something you understand now that you didn't before.

---

## Before You Start

- Your [case-study deck](/presenting/case-study-deck) is finished, and your live link works.
- Your deck is open and ready to share, and your prototype is open in another tab.
- You have somewhere to take notes while others present.

**Presenting with a group online?** Share your screen, and ask one person to keep time.

**Working alone?** Record yourself presenting for 3 minutes, and send the recording to one or two people you trust, with the feedback questions below. You can also do the group discussion on your own, using the prototypes and case studies of other people that you can find online.

---

## Step 3: Present and Give Feedback

### Presenting

1. **Open with what you made,** in one sentence, before you show anything else.
2. **Show your slides,** and move to the live demo when you reach your final prototype. If the demo fails, switch to your screenshots or your [demo video](/presenting/demo-video) and keep going.
3. **Stop at 3 minutes,** even if you haven't finished. Ask someone to keep time for you.
4. **Listen during the conversation.** Write down what people say. You don't need to defend every choice; "I hadn't thought of that" is a good answer.

Practice once beforehand with a timer. Most people have too much for 3 minutes on their first try.

### Giving feedback

Good feedback helps the presenter see their work through someone else's eyes. Two rules:

- **Say what you saw.** Describe what happened before you judge it. "When you typed a long letter, the assistant ignored the word limit" is more useful than "it didn't work well."
- **Be specific.** Point to one slide, one output or one moment. "The before-and-after on slide 5 made the change clear" helps more than "nice deck."

Some openings to try:

- "What I understood you made was…"
- "The moment that stood out was…"
- "I was surprised when…"
- "I wanted to know more about…"
- "Have you tried…?"

Take notes while each person presents. You'll use them in the group discussion.

---

## Step 4: Discuss as a Group

After everyone has presented, talk together about three things. Take about 10 minutes for each.

1. **Themes.** What came up in more than one project? Think about what people built, what broke, and what surprised them.
2. **Insights.** What do you understand now about designing with AI that you didn't at the start?
3. **Next steps.** What will you try next, on your own project or a new one?

Ask one person to write the themes where everyone can see them.

If you're on your own, or want help sorting a long set of notes, paste your notes into an AI chat tool and send:

> Here are my notes from a set of project presentations. Group them into three to five themes. For each theme, name the projects it came from and quote my notes. Don't add themes that aren't in my notes.

Check the themes against your own memory. You were in the room; the tool wasn't.

---

## Checkpoint

- You presented in 3 minutes, and wrote down the feedback you got.
- You gave at least one specific piece of feedback to another presenter.
- You have a short list of themes, insights and next steps.

---

## If Something Goes Wrong

**My live demo won't load.** Switch to your screenshots or demo video straight away. Don't spend your 3 minutes on debugging.

**Nobody says anything in my conversation time.** Ask a question of your own: "Where would you expect this to break?" or "Who else could use this?"

**I ran out of time.** That's common. Cut the slide you rushed through, and try again.

---

## Reflect

- Which piece of feedback will change what you do next?
- Which theme surprised you most?
- What did someone else's project show you about your own?
