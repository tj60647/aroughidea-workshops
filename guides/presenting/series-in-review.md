*This guide covers step 5 of the [TLDR](/presenting/tldr), with more explanation.*

## What This Is

A look back at where you've been. Each workshop added one layer: what a language model does, how to design its behavior, how to build software with it, how to share what you build, and how to keep building with a coding assistant. This guide recaps the five, then offers three ways to think about AI that tie them together.

---

## Words You'll See

- **Context completion engine:** a way to describe a language model: it takes the text it's given, the context, and predicts what comes next.
- **Frame:** a way of looking at something that decides which questions you ask about it.
- **Knowledge artifact:** something made to hold and share knowledge, such as a book, a map or a database.

## Step 5: Look Back Over the Series

### Where you've been

1. **[How It Actually Works: The Context Completion Engine](/how-it-works/).** A language model predicts the next token from its context, over and over: predict, check whether to stop, append, repeat. You met tokens, sampling from a probability distribution, system instructions, model selection and model parameters.
2. **[AI as a Material: Modeling Behavior](/ai-as-material/).** You prototyped an interaction by bodystorming it, used design patterns for system instructions, and tested an agent's behavior in a sandbox.
3. **[AI as a Tool: Generative Software](/ai-as-tool/).** You went from chatbots to vibe coding, and used AI Studio to build software through conversation.
4. **[Sharing AI Prototypes: A Production Pipeline](/sharing-prototypes/).** You published a prototype from AI Studio to GitHub to Vercel, so anyone could open it.
5. **[Augmenting AI Prototypes: The Prototyping Toolkit](https://prototypingtoolkit.aroughidea.com/).** You set up VS Code with a coding assistant, Claude Code or Codex, alongside GitHub and Vercel, and added Playwright, prototyping instructions and, optionally, a backend.

### Three ways to think about AI

- **AI as a tool.** The most familiar frame. You use AI to get things done: to write, to code, to publish.
- **AI as a material.** AI becomes an ingredient in something you create for others. You shape its behavior the way you'd shape wood or type.
- **AI as a medium.** The most expansive frame. AI becomes a new kind of knowledge artifact: something that holds and shares knowledge in a new way.

The frames aren't a ranking. The same project can use all three. Your coding assistant was a tool, the agent inside your prototype was a material, and the prototype itself may point toward a medium.

### Place your own work

Look back through your journal and ask which frame you were in at each stage. An AI chat tool can give you a first pass. Paste your journal and send:

> Here is my process journal. For each workshop entry, tell me whether I was treating AI as a tool, as a material or as a medium, and quote the line that shows it. Where it's unclear, say so.

Disagree with it where you know better. The point is to notice when you moved between frames.
