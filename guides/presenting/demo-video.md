# Making a Demo Video

*This guide covers step 2 of the [TLDR](/presenting/tldr), with more explanation. It's optional, and recommended.*

---

## What This Is

A **demo video** is a short recording of your prototype in use. It works when a live demo doesn't: when the network is slow, when your API key has run out, or when someone looks at your portfolio a year from now.

Keep it to **2 to 3 minutes**. Walk through the system:

- what it does;
- what you gave it: the inputs, such as what the user types and the instructions and knowledge behind it;
- where it breaks.

It should clearly show the working prototype, with its inputs, outputs and interactions.

---

## Words You'll See

- **Screen recording:** a video of what's on your screen, usually with your voice.
- **Script:** what you plan to show and say, written down before you record.
- **Shot:** one continuous piece of a video, such as typing one input and showing its output.
- **Unlisted video:** a video that only people with the link can find.

---

## Before You Start

- Your prototype is working, at its live link or on your computer.
- You know which inputs show it at its best, and one input where it breaks.
- You have a quiet place to record, and a microphone. Your laptop's built-in one is fine.

---

## Step 2: Record a Demo Video

### Write a short script

A script keeps a 3-minute video from turning into 8 minutes. Write a few notes on what your prototype does, what you give it and where it breaks. Then paste them into an AI chat tool, such as ChatGPT, and send:

> Here is what my prototype does, what I give it and where it breaks. Write a script for a demo video of 2 to 3 minutes. Split it into shots, and for each shot say what to show on screen and what to say. Show the working prototype with its inputs and outputs, and include one place where it breaks. Keep my own words where you can.

Edit the script until it sounds like you. Read it aloud once and time it.

A simple order that works:

1. **What it is** (about 20 seconds): the prototype's name, who it's for and what it does.
2. **What you gave it** (about 30 seconds): a glimpse of the system instructions or knowledge, or your system diagram.
3. **It working** (about 60 seconds): one or two inputs and their outputs, typed live.
4. **Where it breaks** (about 30 seconds): an input that goes wrong, and what you think causes it.
5. **What's next** (about 10 seconds): what you'd change, and the link.

### Record it

- **Mac:** press `Cmd + Shift + 5`, choose to record the whole screen or part of it, and click **Record**.
- **Windows:** open **Snipping Tool**, choose the video option, select the area, and click **Start**.

Before you record, close other tabs and turn off notifications. Make the browser text large enough to read in a small video window. Hide any API keys, passwords or personal information.

If a take goes wrong, keep going and fix it in the next take. Two or three takes is normal.

### Share it

Upload the video somewhere that gives you a link, such as YouTube (as an unlisted video) or Google Drive. Check that the link opens in a private browser window, then add it to the last slide of your deck and to your journal.

---

## Checkpoint

- The video is between 2 and 3 minutes long.
- It shows your prototype working, with its inputs and outputs.
- It shows one place where your prototype breaks.
- The link opens for someone who isn't signed in to your account.

---

## If Something Goes Wrong

**The video is too long.** Cut the explanation, not the demo. Viewers learn more from watching the prototype work than from hearing about it.

**My prototype failed while recording.** If it's a new failure, keep it: it may be your "where it breaks" shot. If it's an outage or a spent API key, record your prototype running on your computer instead.

**The link asks viewers to sign in.** Change the sharing setting to "anyone with the link," then test again in a private window.
